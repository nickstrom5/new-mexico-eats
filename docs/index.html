Built around how people here eat

Star ratings won't tell you which drive-in roasts its own chile or which family has run the cafe since 1963. New Mexico Eats is organized the way people here eat: green chile cheeseburgers, breakfast burritos and long-running family restaurants, each place on New Mexico True's own trails and checked in fall 2026. Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out on the high desert.

What stays on your phone

New Mexico Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is New Mexico Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The green chile cheeseburger, breakfast burrito and Culinary Treasures lists start from New Mexico True's own trails, the state tourism department's, each place matched to an open listing at its address in fall 2026; the oldest places carry a dated 2025-26 source. The wider directory of 4,099 restaurants starts from open map listings (Overture Maps), filtered to the kinds that proved reliable where states publish restaurant lists.

Where can I get a green chile cheeseburger?

Our list has 34 stops on New Mexico True's Green Chile Cheeseburger Trail, from the Owl Bar in San Antonio and Sparky's in Hatch to Albuquerque's Central Avenue and Gallup. In the web search, the list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do I check a New Mexico restaurant's inspection?

Albuquerque posts a weekly report of its inspections, and Albuquerque places show the result they've posted (Approved, Conditional Approved, Unsatisfactory or Closed) with its date. NMED and Bernalillo County publish no results online, and tribal land is outside state and city inspection. We compute no grade.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

No. New Mexico's licensee list is a paid records request, so the app doesn't say either way.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
